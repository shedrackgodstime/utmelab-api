# Newton’s Second Law of Motion

**What you’ll learn**
- Define: system, external force, net external force, mass, and acceleration.
- State Newton’s second law in words and as `F_net = m a` (for constant mass), including the direction of acceleration and how force and mass affect it.
- Use `a = F_net / m` to find acceleration in a simple one-dimensional situation.

**Before you start**
- You should know: force (a push or pull), mass, velocity, and that acceleration means a change in velocity.
- You should be comfortable with basic algebra and SI units: newtons (N), kilograms (kg), m/s².

**What this covers**
- Newton’s second law for a chosen system with constant mass in an inertial frame (classical mechanics), using net external force in one dimension/same direction.

**Not covered here**
- Variable-mass systems, rotation, non-inertial frames, relativity/quantum effects, or multi-force 2D/3D vector components beyond net force and direction.

## Core ideas

- **System:** The object or group of objects you choose to analyze.
- **External force:** A force acting on the system from outside it. (Internal forces act between parts within the system.)
- **Net external force (`F_net`):** The vector sum of all external forces on the system—the overall unbalanced external force. Direction matters.
- **Mass (`m`):** A measure of inertia—how much the system resists changes in motion. In this topic, mass is constant. Mass is not the same as weight.
- **Weight:** The gravitational force on an object (often `W = m g` near Earth). Weight is a force (N); mass is inertia (kg).
- **Acceleration (`a`):** The rate of change of velocity (change in speed and/or direction). It is a vector.

## Explanation

Newton’s second law describes how a system’s motion changes when external forces act on it.

1. **Choose the system.** Decide what you are analyzing, then identify the external forces on it. External forces determine the acceleration of the system as a whole.
2. **Find the net external force.** Combine all external forces into `F_net`. If they balance, `F_net = 0`; if they do not, there is a nonzero net force in some direction.
3. **Relate force, mass, and acceleration** (constant mass, inertial frame):
   - Acceleration is in the **same direction** as `F_net`.
   - Acceleration is **directly proportional** to `F_net` (greater `F_net`, same `m` → greater `a`).
   - Acceleration is **inversely proportional** to mass (greater `m`, same `F_net` → smaller `a`).

**In words:** The acceleration of a system is directly proportional to, and in the same direction as, the net external force acting on the system, and inversely proportional to its mass.

**In symbols:**
- `a = F_net / m`
- `F_net = m a`

**Units:** `1 N = 1 kg·m/s²`.

**Common confusions**
- Using one force instead of the **net external** force.
- Mixing up **mass** (kg, inertia) and **weight** (N, a force).
- Forgetting that acceleration points in the **direction of `F_net`**.

## Worked example

**Problem:** A 5.0 kg crate on a smooth horizontal surface is pulled so that the net horizontal external force on it is 20 N to the right. Find the crate’s acceleration.

**Given:** `m = 5.0 kg`, `F_net = 20 N` (right)  
**Find:** `a`

**Calculation:**  
`a = F_net / m = 20 N / 5.0 kg = 4.0 m/s²`

**Answer:** The crate accelerates at **4.0 m/s² to the right**.

**Check:** Larger net force (same mass) → larger acceleration; larger mass (same net force) → smaller acceleration.

## Check your understanding

1. A 2.0 kg object experiences a net external force of 10 N to the east. What is its acceleration (magnitude and direction)?
2. The same net external force acts first on a cart and then on a second cart with twice the mass. How does the acceleration of the second cart compare with the first?
3. Can an object have forces acting on it and still have zero acceleration? Explain using `F_net`.

**Answers**
1. `a = 10 N / 2.0 kg = 5.0 m/s²` to the east.
2. Half as large (acceleration is inversely proportional to mass for the same net force).
3. Yes. If the external forces balance, `F_net = 0`, so `a = 0` in that direction.

## Key points

- Analyze a chosen **system** and use the **net external force** on it.
- `F_net = m a` and `a = F_net / m` (constant mass, inertial frame).
- Acceleration is in the **same direction** as `F_net`.
- Directly proportional to `F_net`; inversely proportional to `m`.
- Mass ≠ weight. If `F_net = 0`, then `a = 0` (in that direction).
