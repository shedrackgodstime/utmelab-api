# Audit (working): English (language/usage samples), against scope-definition.md

Status: draft in `openlearn/`. No migration. Samples from `utmelab-bank/data/concepts/eng_*.json`.

## Samples examined

- `eng_emphatic_contrastive_stress.json` — “Emphatic and Contrastive Stress in English” (teaching text about sentence/emphatic/contrastive stress patterns)
- `eng_commonly_confused_words.json` — “Commonly Confused Words and Usage” (learning objectives, description of commonly confused words)

## 1) What are the knowledge entities?

- **Concepts**: sentence stress, emphatic stress, contrastive stress, content words vs function words (categories), commonly confused words (a class), specific word pairs/examples mentioned (implied). Also notions like “meaning signaled by stress”.
- **Statements**: claims such as “In English, stress in sentences helps signal meaning…”; “Content words … are usually stressed, while function words…”; “commonly confused words … share similar pronunciation, meaning, or spelling…” (descriptive statements about English usage/norms in a teaching scope).
- **Procedures**: identification/usage strategies implied (“Identify commonly confused words. Use strategies…” as objectives) — procedural/skill-like guidance in teaching form.
- **Models**: not obvious as formal models; more like categorizations and descriptive patterns (a loose descriptive “model” of stress behavior) — borderline.
- **Relations**: category relations (content words include nouns/main verbs/adjectives/adverbs/question words; function words include pronouns/prepositions etc.), contrast relations implied between stress types.

## 2) Which primitives do they use?

Concept and Statement appear clearly (categories and descriptive claims). Procedure appears as objectives/strategies (teaching phrased). Model is weak/implicit (patterns, not a formal model structure). Relations are mostly categorical/contrastive, stated informally.

## 3) What are their scopes?

Scope is English language usage/description in an educational (often exam/skills) context (titles reference GST/listening contexts in other files). These are descriptive claims about English in a specified variety/context of instruction, not universal physical laws; scope matters (variety, register may be implied). Subject label `english_language` is a view; constitutive scope is the language/teaching framework described.

## 4) How are they related?

Category membership (“content words such as…”), contrast between emphatic vs contrastive stress, cause/meaning links (“helps signal meaning”). Mostly informal prose relations.

## 5) What belongs in Study rather than Knowledge?

Much of this material reads as Study/teaching: learning objectives, explanatory phrasing, examples-as-instruction. Knowledge candidates are the descriptive statements about patterns/categories (e.g. which classes are typically stressed) and defined notions (what emphatic vs contrastive stress means in that framework) — but even definitions here are pedagogical descriptions. The line is softer than in physics: linguistic “facts” in teaching texts are often presented normatively/descriptively for learners. Still, scope-definition test applies: if it states a descriptive claim about the language in that scope, it is a knowledge-leaning Statement; if it tells you how to learn it, it is Study.

## 6) Does anything resist the five primitives?

- **Norms/usage rules vs empirical claims**: “usually stressed” is a descriptive/generalization in usage, not a hard law; status is different from physics laws. The Statement primitive can carry status, but the nature of evidence/normativity is different.
- **Categories as entities**: “content words”, “function words” are Concept-like categories; sometimes treated as classifications (could be seen as Model/components of a classification scheme). Primitive fit is okay if called Concepts or noted as classification roles.
- **Highly pedagogical framing**: many sentences are instruction (“Identify…”, “Use strategies…”) which are Study/Procedure-presentation; extracting pure knowledge requires separating the claim from the teaching prompt. This is a stronger Study/Knowledge mix than the physics samples in form.
- No obvious missing primitive, but the domain is more about conventions, categories, and strategies than about formal models/laws in the same way. The five primitives still apply as lenses, with care about status (normative/descriptive) and the Study-heavy packaging.
